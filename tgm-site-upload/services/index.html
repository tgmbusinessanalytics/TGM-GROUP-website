Five areas of work, two practices

Most engagements start in one of these and touch a second. The constraint is rarely where the symptom is, so we would rather scope the work after the diagnosis than before it.

  • Business growth
  • Operational excellence
  • AI & automation
  • Strategic execution
  • Leadership & capability

Business growth

TGM Business Services

Most growth plans fail on delivery rather than demand. An organisation wins the larger contract and then discovers the process that worked at ten clients does not work at forty, that nobody owns onboarding, and that the founder is the only person who can resolve an exception.

We start by working out where revenue genuinely comes from — not the story, the actual distribution across clients, offers and channels. That usually reframes the question. The organisation that thinks it has a lead generation problem frequently has a conversion problem, a pricing problem, or a retention problem that is quietly cancelling out every new client won.

From there the work is specific: define the offer, set a price you can defend, build a sales process that runs without heroics, and fix the operational constraints that will bite at the new volume before you get there rather than after.

This work sits with TGM Business Services and usually runs alongside operational excellence, because the two failure modes are the same failure mode.

What you end up with

  • A written view of where revenue is actually coming from, and what it costs to get
  • A defined offer and pricing position you can hold in a negotiation
  • A sales process the team follows without being chased
  • The operational changes needed before volume increases, identified in advance

Operational excellence

TGM Business Services

Every organisation has a process that exists on paper and a process that happens. The gap between them is where margin goes.

We map the real one. That means sitting with the people doing the work, walking a job through end to end, and finding the steps that were never designed — the spreadsheet someone maintains privately, the approval that waits for one person’s inbox, the rework that has become normal enough that nobody counts it any more.

Then we find the constraint. Not all the problems; the one that is actually limiting throughput. Fixing anything else first feels productive and changes nothing. Once it is named and measured, the redesign is usually smaller than expected — a handful of decisions about ownership, sequence and what stops being done at all.

The last part matters most. A process that only works while a consultant is in the building has not been improved, it has been temporarily supervised. We build with the people who will own it, and we document it in their words.

This work sits with TGM Business Services.

What you end up with

  • A mapped view of how the work actually flows, including the undocumented steps
  • The constraint named, measured and prioritised against the others
  • A redesigned process with clear ownership at every handoff
  • Documentation the team wrote and will therefore use

AI & automation

TGM Business Analytics

The question is not whether AI is useful. It is which part of your organisation would benefit, at what cost, and whether the underlying process is stable enough to automate at all.

Automating a broken process makes it break faster. So the assessment starts with the work itself: what is manual, how many hours it consumes, how often it goes wrong, and what happens downstream when it does. Some of what surfaces needs no AI whatsoever — a report that takes two days a month is often a data structure problem rather than an intelligence problem, and the cheap fix is the right one.

What remains is a shortlist with real numbers against it. Where AI genuinely fits — document handling, classification, drafting, summarising volumes of text nobody has time to read — we build it, put it in production, and make sure somebody inside the organisation understands it well enough to change it. Automation you cannot modify is a dependency, not an asset.

We will also tell you when the answer is no. A tool that saves four hours a month and costs a day a month to maintain is not a saving.

This work sits with TGM Business Analytics.

What you end up with

  • An inventory of manual work, ranked by hours consumed and error rate
  • A shortlist of automation and AI opportunities with an honest cost and effort estimate
  • What to automate now, what to fix first, and what to leave alone
  • Working automation in production, with the team able to change it

Strategic execution

TGM Business Services

Strategy is rarely the problem. Most organisations know roughly what they should be doing. What is missing is the mechanism that turns that into changed behaviour across a few hundred working days.

Execution work is unglamorous and specific. Break the strategy into initiatives small enough to finish. Give each one a named owner who has the authority to make the decisions it requires. Sequence them so the dependencies run in the right order rather than in parallel. Measure the outcome, not the meeting.

The part organisations find hardest is stopping. An initiative that is not working consumes the same attention as one that is, and without explicit stop criteria agreed at the start, it usually continues until someone senior loses patience. We set those criteria up front, while it is still an abstract question.

We also keep the governance light. A monthly forum that takes ninety minutes and produces decisions beats a weekly one that produces status updates.

This work sits with TGM Business Services.

What you end up with

  • The strategy translated into a sequenced set of initiatives with named owners
  • A measurement framework that shows progress rather than activity
  • A governance rhythm short enough that people attend it
  • Explicit stop criteria for every initiative

Leadership & capability

TGM Business Services

There is a size at which an organisation stops being able to run on the founder’s judgement. It is usually reached before anybody notices, and the symptom is a leadership team that is busy but not deciding.

The work starts with decision rights: which decisions are being made at which level, which ones are escalating that should not, and which are being avoided altogether. That is a structural question more often than a personnel one. Managers escalate by default when it has never been made explicit what they are allowed to decide alone.

From there it is practical capability. How to run a meeting that ends in decisions. How to give feedback that changes something. How to hold a number without micromanaging the person holding it. How to hire for a role you have never done yourself.

We also look at concentration risk honestly — the roles where one person’s resignation would cost the organisation months, and what to do about that before it happens rather than during.

This work sits with TGM Business Services and is often the phase-four work that makes everything else hold.

What you end up with

  • A clear view of which decisions sit at which level, and which are stuck
  • Managers equipped to run their own area without escalating by default
  • A meeting and reporting rhythm that surfaces problems early
  • A succession view for the roles the organisation cannot afford to lose

Ninety minutes. A straight answer about what is actually holding you back.
