Strategy is rarely the problem. Most organisations know roughly what they should
be doing. What is missing is the mechanism that turns that into changed
behaviour across a few hundred working days.

Execution work is unglamorous and specific. Break the strategy into initiatives
small enough to finish. Give each one a named owner who has the authority to
make the decisions it requires. Sequence them so the dependencies run in the
right order rather than in parallel. Measure the outcome, not the meeting.

The part organisations find hardest is stopping. An initiative that is not
working consumes the same attention as one that is, and without explicit stop
criteria agreed at the start, it usually continues until someone senior loses
patience. We set those criteria up front, while it is still an abstract
question.

We also keep the governance light. A monthly forum that takes ninety minutes
and produces decisions beats a weekly one that produces status updates.
